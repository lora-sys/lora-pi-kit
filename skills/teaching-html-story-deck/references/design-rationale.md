# Design Rationale

This file records the reasoning learned while creating and improving the Shape Up Skill teaching deck.

## Why start with the problem

Starting with the Skill feels promotional. Starting with the failure mode gives the audience a reason to care.

The reveal becomes stronger after the audience understands:

- AI makes execution cheap;
- wrong projects also become cheap to start;
- judgment and boundaries are therefore scarce.

## Why use dark editorial design

The audience is developer-oriented. Near-black surfaces, large type, restrained panels, and one luminous accent provide:

- focus during screen recording;
- clear hierarchy;
- compatibility with technical diagrams;
- modern tool-building atmosphere;
- visual sophistication without decorative noise.

## Why inline SVG

Inline SVG:

- stays in the single file;
- scales cleanly;
- supports readable labels;
- supports semantic path animation;
- requires no image hosting;
- can be generated for the current topic.

## Why use both mind maps and architecture diagrams

They answer different questions:

- mind map: what capabilities belong to the system?
- architecture: how do roles, data, and authority interact?

Do not merge them into one dense image.

## Why include a trigger matrix

A reusable Skill must know when not to activate. A matrix makes the boundary teachable:

- small direct tasks remain direct;
- unclear expensive projects require shaping;
- high uncertainty may require a research bet.

## Why reveal the Skill last

The final page converts the method into a usable tool. The Skill feels like the logical implementation of the teaching, not an unrelated advertisement.

## Why include presenter notes

The HTML is for live teaching and screen recording. A speaker needs the purpose, transition, main sentence, and protection against over-explaining.

## Sources of inspiration

The design borrows broad qualities, not exact assets:

- Linear-like precision and restrained interaction;
- Vercel-like monochrome contrast and developer-product clarity;
- editorial poster thinking for strong hierarchy;
- system-design diagrams for causal explanation;
- Shape Up’s Hill Chart and bounded-bet reasoning.

Never copy brand logos, proprietary illustrations, or exact layouts.
